# Playbook: Negotiation

## Overview

Negotiation in sales is not about winning or pressuring — it's about finding an arrangement that works for both sides while protecting the value you've established. Good preparation is the single most important factor. This playbook covers preparation, execution principles, and how to reach a durable agreement.

## When to use

- A prospect has asked for a price concession or contract change
- You're preparing for a formal commercial conversation
- A deal is at the proposal or legal review stage

## When NOT to use

- Value hasn't been established yet (go back to discovery and demo)
- You don't know your walk-away constraints (get them from your manager first)

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] Value established and confirmed (prospect has stated the business impact)
- [ ] Walk-away constraints known (minimum acceptable price, non-negotiable terms)
- [ ] Approval requirements known (what can you approve independently vs. what needs sign-off?)
- [ ] Options prepared (3 scenarios: optimal, acceptable, last resort)
- [ ] Trade-off variables identified (price, term length, scope, payment terms, onboarding, support tier)

## Step-by-step guide

### Step 1: Prepare your options
Use the Negotiation Planner skill. Prepare at least three scenarios:
- **Optimal:** Best outcome for both sides (not just for you)
- **Acceptable:** Meets your commercial floor and their core ask
- **Last resort:** Walk-away conditions — be clear about what these are before the conversation

Identify which variables are tradeable (e.g., payment terms, implementation timeline, contract length) vs. non-negotiable (e.g., minimum price, core terms).

### Step 2: Anchor on value, not price
Before discussing price, confirm the value again. "You mentioned this would eliminate 2 FTE of manual work per week — how are you thinking about the value of that?" Anchoring on value before price makes price concessions feel proportionate.

### Step 3: Respond to the first ask carefully
When they make their first ask ("Can you do better on price?"), don't concede immediately. Ask: "Help me understand — is price the main concern, or is there something else about the package that's not working?" Often the ask is a proxy for something else.

### Step 4: Trade, don't just concede
If you move on price, get something in return. Examples: longer contract term, faster decision timeline, reference agreement, reduced scope. Every concession should be tied to a trade.

### Step 5: Confirm terms before finalising
Before the deal is done, summarise the agreed terms in writing. "To confirm: [terms]. Does that accurately capture what we agreed?" Never assume agreement — confirm it.

### Step 6: Know when to walk away
If the deal requires you to go below your walk-away constraints, the right answer is to walk away cleanly. A deal below your minimum is usually worse than no deal.

## Common mistakes

1. **Conceding before anchoring on value.** If you discount before the prospect has confirmed the value, you're teaching them that your price doesn't reflect your value.
2. **Not knowing your walk-away.** Without a clear walk-away, you'll accept a deal you shouldn't. Get approval thresholds from your manager before the conversation.
3. **Treating negotiation as adversarial.** A deal that only benefits you doesn't close. A deal that only benefits them creates churn. Good negotiation finds a durable agreement.
4. **Making verbal concessions without confirming in writing.** Always summarise agreed terms in a written message after the conversation.
5. **Negotiating without authority.** If you need approval for something, say so. "Let me confirm I can do that" is not a weakness; it's honest.

## Example scenario

**Northlight Technologies** asks for a 20% discount at the proposal stage. AE checks walk-away constraint: maximum 12% discount without VP approval.

Response: "I appreciate you sharing that — before I look at what's possible, can I understand what's driving the budget constraint? Is it the total contract value or the monthly commitment?" → CFO has a flat budget ceiling for new tools.

Trade offer: "I can't get to 20%, but I can offer 10% if you commit to a 24-month term instead of 12. That gives you a lower annual spend and predictability." → Northlight agrees.

Terms confirmed in email same day.
