# Playbook: Sales Manager Coaching

## Overview

Coaching is the highest-leverage activity a sales manager can do. This playbook covers how to structure 1:1s, deal reviews, and skills coaching so that conversations are evidence-based, specific, and actionable — rather than vague encouragement or micromanagement.

## When to use

- Preparing for weekly 1:1s with AEs or SDRs
- Running a deal review with a rep
- Developing a coaching plan for a specific skill gap

## When NOT to use

- Managing underperformance (that's a separate HR/management process)
- Running a pipeline review meeting (use Weekly Pipeline Review workflow)

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] Rep's current pipeline reviewed
- [ ] Recent call notes or activity reviewed (if available)
- [ ] Previous 1:1 notes reviewed
- [ ] Specific deal(s) to review identified
- [ ] Coaching goal for this session defined (one thing to work on)

---

## Step-by-step guide

### Before the 1:1: prepare evidence
Don't run a 1:1 from memory. Review:
- The rep's pipeline (what's moved, what's stalled, what's missing)
- Recent deals (what patterns do you see?)
- Their self-assessment (ask them to rate themselves — it reveals what they know vs. don't know)

Use the Sales Manager 1:1 Prep skill to structure your preparation.

### Opening the 1:1
Start with their agenda, not yours. "What's most on your mind this week?" This surfaces what they're thinking about and is often more useful than your prepared agenda.

### Pipeline review (10 minutes)
Walk through 2–3 deals. Use evidence-seeking questions:
- "What makes you confident about this close date?"
- "Who's the economic buyer — have you spoken to them?"
- "What's the one thing that could kill this deal?"

The goal is to surface assumptions, not interrogate. If you catch an unsupported assumption, ask: "How do you know that?" rather than "That's wrong."

### Skill coaching (10–15 minutes)
Choose one skill area to focus on, based on what you're seeing in their pipeline and calls. Common areas:
- Qualification depth (are they leaving discovery calls with enough information?)
- Multithreading (are they talking to only one stakeholder?)
- Outreach personalisation (are their messages generic?)
- Objection handling (are they pushing through or understanding objections?)

Use a specific example from their recent work. Not "you need to get better at qualification" but "in the Northlight call notes, you have the champion but no economic buyer identified — let's talk about how to get to the CFO."

### Close with one action
End every 1:1 with one specific action the rep will take before your next meeting. Not a list. One thing. Confirm what it is and when.

## Common mistakes

1. **Running the 1:1 as a pipeline review.** Pipeline reviews should be separate. 1:1s are about the person and their development.
2. **Coaching on everything at once.** One coaching focus per session is more effective than five.
3. **Using judgment without evidence.** "You need to qualify better" is opinion. "Your last 3 deals had no economic buyer engaged" is data.
4. **Talking more than the rep.** A coaching session where the manager talks 70% of the time is a lecture, not a coaching conversation.
5. **No follow-through.** If you agree on an action and never check back, reps learn the actions don't matter.

## Example scenario

**Sales Manager** prepares for 1:1 with AE responsible for Northlight Technologies deal.

Preparation: pipeline shows Northlight at 60 days, CFO not yet engaged, close date set for Q4 without supporting evidence.

1:1: "What's your biggest concern about Northlight right now?" → AE: "I haven't managed to get the CFO introduced yet."

Coaching: "Let's work through that together. In your champion relationship with the VP Ops — have you asked them directly to make the introduction, or have you been waiting?" → AE: "Waiting." → Role-play the ask.

Action: "Before our next 1:1, ask the VP Ops for a CFO introduction — specific date, specific ask. Can you do that this week?" → yes.

## Success indicators

- Reps leave 1:1s with a specific action and know why it matters
- Pipeline quality improves over a quarter (more fields populated, fewer stalled deals)
- Reps can articulate their own coaching focus area unprompted
