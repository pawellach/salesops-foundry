# Playbook: Objection Handling

## Overview

An objection is a signal, not a stop sign. It tells you what the prospect is worried about or hasn't understood yet. Good objection handling means first understanding the objection correctly — what type is it? what's the underlying concern? — and then responding with honesty and evidence, not spin or pressure.

## When to use

- Before a call (preparing for anticipated objections)
- After a call (reviewing objections that came up and planning responses)
- Training new reps on handling common objections

## When NOT to use

- To develop manipulative or high-pressure responses (this playbook produces ethical, evidence-based responses only)

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] Discovery notes reviewed (what concerns did they raise?)
- [ ] Known objection types mapped for this prospect/stage
- [ ] Evidence prepared for factual responses (verified, not fabricated)
- [ ] Responses reviewed before use

---

## Step-by-step guide

### Step 1: Classify the objection
Use the Objection Classifier skill. Most objections fall into one of these types:
- **Misunderstanding:** They think your product can't do something it can, or believe something false about your offer.
- **Lack of priority:** "Not right now" — they have competing priorities, not a permanent no.
- **Value question:** "I'm not sure this is worth it" — they haven't seen a clear connection to their specific business impact.
- **Stakeholder concern:** "My IT/Legal/CFO will push back" — a proxy objection from someone else.
- **Competitive preference:** "We're already looking at [Competitor]" — they have an alternative.
- **Genuine disqualifier:** The objection reflects a real mismatch. The right response is to acknowledge it.

### Step 2: Clarify before responding
Ask a follow-up question before responding. "Help me understand — is it more about timing or about whether this would work for your environment?" You can't respond to an objection you haven't fully understood.

### Step 3: Respond with evidence, not pressure
For each objection type:
- **Misunderstanding:** Correct with a demonstration or specific capability reference.
- **Priority:** Acknowledge and agree on a check-in date. Don't push.
- **Value:** Return to their stated impact. "You mentioned the reconciliation delays cost 2 FTE weekly — how are you thinking about the cost of continuing that?"
- **Stakeholder concern:** Offer to help address it. "Would it help if I put together a security overview for your IT team?"
- **Competitive:** Acknowledge, don't attack. Focus on fit, not win/loss framing.
- **Genuine disqualifier:** Acknowledge it honestly. Sometimes the right answer is "you're right, this probably isn't the right fit."

### Step 4: Confirm the objection is addressed
After your response, ask: "Does that address your concern, or is there still something I haven't covered?"

## Common mistakes

1. **Responding before understanding.** Giving a canned response to an objection you haven't classified is a missed opportunity to learn something important.
2. **Pushing through a genuine disqualifier.** If the objection reflects a real mismatch, the ethical and practical response is to acknowledge it. Pushing through wastes both parties' time and damages trust.
3. **Using social proof to override concerns.** "Lots of companies in your situation use us" doesn't address the specific concern.
4. **Not confirming the objection was addressed.** Always close the loop.
5. **Treating objections as adversarial.** The prospect isn't your opponent. They're trying to make a good decision.

## Example scenario

**During the Northlight Technologies demo**, the CTO raises: "We'd need to validate this with our security team before proceeding — they have strict requirements for vendor integrations."

Classification: Stakeholder concern (IT/Security gatekeeper).

Response: "That's completely understandable, and it's good to surface this early. We've worked through security reviews with similar companies — I can put together a pre-fill for your security questionnaire and set up a technical call with your team. Would that be the right way to handle it?"

Objection confirmed addressed: CTO agrees on a technical call next week.

## Success indicators

- Objections surfaced and addressed in discovery (before they kill the deal)
- Objections correctly classified (not guessed)
- Post-call notes document each objection type and the response given
