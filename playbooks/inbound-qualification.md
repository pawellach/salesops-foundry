# Playbook: Inbound Qualification

## Overview

Inbound leads arrive with varying quality, context, and intent. Qualification means determining — quickly and accurately — whether an inbound inquiry represents a real opportunity worth pursuing, and if so, what the right next step is. Moving too slowly wastes buying windows; moving too fast without qualification wastes everyone's time.

## When to use

- A new inbound lead has arrived (form, email, event)
- You want to triage a batch of inbound leads
- You're deciding whether to fast-track or slow-track a specific lead

## When NOT to use

- Lead is clearly outside ICP with no exception case (disqualify, don't qualify)
- Lead is actually a referral from a known champion (treat as warm; skip formal triage)

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] ICP criteria defined
- [ ] Qualification framework selected (MEDDICC, SPICED, BANT, or custom)
- [ ] Lead routing rules known
- [ ] Response template ready for approved send

---

## Step-by-step guide

### Step 1: Capture and review the lead record
Before doing anything else, read what you have. What did they request? What company? What role? What did they say (or not say)?

### Step 2: Check for obvious disqualifiers
Does the company or role meet minimum ICP criteria? If not, this is a disqualify — don't invest time in qualification.

### Step 3: Score fit
Use Account Fit Scoring. Even for inbound, fit matters. A high-intent lead from a poor-fit company may not be worth the full sales motion.

### Step 4: Enrich if needed
If the lead record is incomplete (no company, no role, no context), either ask one clarifying question in your response, or do a quick public-source research pass to fill gaps before your first call.

### Step 5: Apply your qualification framework early
Use the Qualification Framework Mapper. For inbound, you typically have: stated pain (from their form/email), some intent signals (they came to you), but unknown: budget, decision process, stakeholders. Note what you have and what you need to learn in the discovery call.

### Step 6: Determine next step
- High fit + high intent → fast-track to discovery call (respond within same business day)
- High fit + unclear intent → respond with a qualifying question before booking
- Medium fit → route to standard process; respond within 24 hours
- Low fit → disqualify or route to a lower-touch process

### Step 7: Draft and approve response
Prepare your first response. Get it reviewed. Send it.

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating all inbound as high priority.** Inbound intent doesn't equal ICP fit. Score both.
2. **Asking too many qualifying questions in the first email.** One question max in the first response. Save the rest for the call.
3. **Delaying response on high-intent leads.** Same-day response on high-fit, high-intent inbound meaningfully improves conversion. Don't wait.
4. **Skipping disqualification.** Routing a poor-fit lead to discovery "just in case" is expensive for both sides.
5. **Accepting stated intent at face value.** "We're looking for a solution" doesn't mean "we have budget and authority." Qualify properly.

## Example scenario

**Meridian Logistics** (fictional, 400 employees) submits a demo request. Contact: Jamie Okafor, Head of Logistics Technology.

Fit score: 5/5. Intent: explicit (demo request). Data completeness: good.

No disqualifiers. Fast-track to discovery. Response drafted: confirms receipt, proposes 3 time slots for 30-min discovery call, references Meridian's logistics context. Sent same business day after rep review.
