# Playbook: Discovery Call

## Overview

Discovery is the most important call in the sales process. Done well, it gives you the information to qualify, personalise, and advance the deal. Done poorly, you move forward blind, over-promise, or lose the deal to a competitor who listened better. This playbook covers preparation, execution, and post-call processing.

## When to use

- First substantive conversation with a prospect (beyond initial intro)
- Re-discovery on a stalled deal
- Discovery within a larger account (new stakeholder, new business unit)

## When NOT to use

- You already have a fully qualified opportunity (this is not a "check-in" call playbook)
- The call is a product demo (use Demo Preparation playbook)

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] Account brief reviewed
- [ ] Prior interaction history reviewed
- [ ] Discovery call plan prepared (objectives, hypotheses, time allocation)
- [ ] Question sequence planned (value-ordered)
- [ ] Qualification framework selected
- [ ] Notes template open and ready

---

## Step-by-step guide

### Before the call
Use the Discovery Call Plan skill to define: your 2–3 learning objectives, your pain hypothesis, the buying committee members who should be on the call (and who isn't), and your time allocation. Prepare questions in priority order — you won't get through all of them.

### Opening (first 3–5 minutes)
- Confirm the agenda and time available
- Ask for their permission to ask questions ("I'd love to understand your situation better — is it OK if I ask some questions?")
- Establish who's on the call and their roles

### Pain discovery (15–20 minutes)
Ask about the current situation before asking about the problem. Let them describe it in their own words. Listen for:
- Specific operational pain (process, system, cost, time)
- Business impact (what happens because of the pain?)
- Priority (is this their top concern right now?)

Do not pitch during discovery. Your job is to learn, not to demonstrate.

### Qualification (10 minutes)
Work through your framework questions naturally — not as a checklist. Key things to understand:
- What would a successful solution look like?
- Who else is involved in this decision?
- What's the timeline and what's driving it?
- What happens if they do nothing?

### Wrap-up (5 minutes)
- Summarise what you heard
- Confirm their next step (what do they need to move forward?)
- Agree on a concrete next action with a date

### After the call (same day)
Use the Call Notes Structurer skill to process your rough notes into the canonical format. Run Pain and Impact Mapper and update your qualification scorecard.

## Common mistakes

1. **Pitching before discovering.** If you explain what your product does before you understand their problem, you're guessing at relevance.
2. **Asking leading questions.** "You must find it frustrating that your current system doesn't scale, right?" tells them what to say. Ask open questions.
3. **Moving to the demo too fast.** A 20-minute discovery call is rarely enough. Book a dedicated discovery call before showing the product.
4. **Forgetting to identify other stakeholders.** If you only talk to one person, you don't know who else is involved. Ask.
5. **Not confirming next steps.** A good call with no agreed next step is a wasted call.

## Example scenario

**AE at Acme Corp** calls Northlight Technologies VP Operations. Objective: confirm the operational data pain hypothesis and identify buying committee.

Opens with agenda confirmation. Asks about current state of operational reporting. VP Operations describes 2-FTE manual reconciliation process across 3 systems. AE listens, asks follow-ups: "What's the impact of the delays that creates?" → "We miss weekly KPI reporting deadlines 40% of the time."

Qualification: Q1 timeline confirmed. CFO not yet aware (gap). Decision: IT and Procurement both involved. Champion: VP Operations.

Wrap-up: "The next step I'd suggest is a technical overview with your IT team and a brief intro to your CFO — does that make sense?" → agreed.

Post-call: notes structured, pain mapped (reconciliation → KPI reporting delays → business decision latency), qualification scorecard updated.
