# Playbook: Demo Preparation

## Overview

A demo that doesn't connect product capabilities to confirmed prospect needs is a product tour, not a demo. This playbook covers how to prepare a tailored, evidence-based demonstration that speaks to what the prospect told you matters to them — not what you think should matter.

## When to use

- Demo has been agreed and is scheduled within 1–2 weeks
- Post-discovery, you have confirmed needs to ground the demo in
- You're refreshing a demo for a stalled deal

## When NOT to use

- Discovery has not been completed (complete it first)
- You're running a generic product overview (this playbook is for tailored demos)

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] Discovery notes processed and pain points confirmed
- [ ] Buyer committee known (who will attend the demo?)
- [ ] Demo readiness review completed
- [ ] Demo environment set up with relevant configuration
- [ ] Storyline built around confirmed needs
- [ ] Any proof points (case studies, data) verified and appropriate to share

---

## Step-by-step guide

### Step 1: Review discovery notes
Re-read your structured discovery notes. The demo must address the specific pains, impacts, and success criteria the prospect named — not what you think they care about.

### Step 2: Run Demo Readiness Review
Use the skill to check whether you're ready. Flags to address before the demo:
- Missing economic buyer attendance
- Unresolved security or technical questions
- Unconfirmed decision criteria
- No agreed demo agenda sent in advance

### Step 3: Build the storyline
Use the Demo Storyline Builder skill. Structure it as:
1. Opening frame: acknowledge their situation (what you learned in discovery)
2. Core demo: show how your product addresses each confirmed pain, in their order of priority
3. Proof: relevant evidence (with permission to share)
4. Close: proposed mutual action plan

### Step 4: Prepare for objections
Review the Objection Classifier skill. Anticipate the 2–3 most likely objections based on discovery and prepare responses.

### Step 5: Send the agenda in advance
Send a brief agenda to attendees 24–48 hours before. Confirm: time, attendees, any materials needed from their side.

### Step 6: Demo environment check
Verify the demo environment works. Use realistic but fictional data. Do not use other customers' data in demos.

## Common mistakes

1. **Demoing all features.** Show only what's relevant to their confirmed needs. Every irrelevant feature adds noise and dilutes the message.
2. **Using another customer's data in the demo environment.** Always use fictional or sanitised data.
3. **Not sending the agenda in advance.** Prospects who know what to expect show up ready to engage.
4. **Skipping the readiness review.** Proceeding without a confirmed economic buyer or with unresolved blockers is a risk you can often avoid.
5. **Not planning the close.** The demo ending should transition naturally to the next step, not trail off.

## Example scenario

**Acme Corp demo to Northlight Technologies.** Discovery confirmed: manual reconciliation pain, Q1 timeline, IT security review needed.

Storyline: Open with "Based on what you told us — 2 FTE on manual reconciliation, 40% of KPI reports delayed — here's how we'd address that." Demo: show automated data consolidation for the 3 systems named. Proof: anonymised benchmark (with permission to reference, no customer names). Close: propose MAP with IT security review timeline and CFO intro.

Readiness check: CFO not attending (noted risk, mitigated by agreeing to a separate CFO briefing this week).

## Success indicators

- Prospect references specific features that match their stated needs during or after the demo
- Mutual action plan agreed before the call ends
- Demo-to-next-step conversion rate (tracked over time)
